AI Tutor — Vision-Aware RAG for Course Materials

Python Gemini 2.0 Flash RAG ChromaDB BM25 + Vector Hybrid Cross-Encoder Rerank Vision LLM FastAPI

Problem Statement

Traditional RAG pipelines strip academic materials down to plain text — which throws away the information that matters most in STEM: diagrams, equations, plots, and slide layouts. Students asking "explain this figure" or "what did the professor mean on slide 12?" get generic answers because the retrieval layer never saw the visuals in the first place.

I built AI Tutor to fix that: a course-aware assistant that ingests real lectures (PDFs and PowerPoints), understands both their text and their visuals, and grounds every answer in a specific slide of a specific lecture — with citations you can verify.

Approach

Most RAG demos stop at "chunk, embed, retrieve, prompt." That works for prose and breaks the moment a slide is 80% diagram. AI Tutor takes a different route: pages with low text density get routed to a vision LLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash) that generates a rich textual description of what's on the slide — the axes of a plot, the arrows in an architecture diagram, the terms in an equation. Those descriptions become first-class retrievable chunks alongside the extracted text.

Retrieval is hybrid by design. BM25 catches exact technical terms ("softmax", "L1 regularization", proper nouns) that dense embeddings often smear together. Vector search catches paraphrases and conceptual similarity. Both ranked lists are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, then a cross-encoder reranker sharpens the top candidates before they reach the LLM. Every generated answer cites its sources as [Lecture X, Slide Y].

🎯 Design goal: A student should never get a confident answer the system can't back up with a specific slide. Grounding > fluency.

What the System Delivers

  1. Vision-aware ingestion. PDFs are parsed with Marker (LaTeX preserved), PowerPoints with python-pptx. Each page is also rendered to an image; pages with low text density are handed to Gemini Vision, which generates a descriptive caption. Text and vision captions are chunked and embedded together.
  2. Hierarchical summarization. Each lecture gets a lecture-level summary. Related lectures are further summarized into cross-lecture topic summaries, so the retriever can answer "how does this connect to last week's material?" without pulling every raw chunk.
  3. Hybrid retrieval with reranking. BM25 and vector search run in parallel, their rankings merged via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and a cross-encoder reranks the top candidates before anything reaches the LLM.
  4. Grounded tutor answers. Gemini generates responses constrained to retrieved context, and every claim carries a [Lecture X, Slide Y] citation the student can click back to.
  5. Quiz generator. The same retrieval pipeline powers automatic MCQ and short-answer quiz generation, with a validation step that checks each question against source slides and guarantees coverage across the selected lectures.

Challenges

Slide-heavy content breaks naive chunking. A single slide can contain a title, a diagram, and three bullet points that only make sense together. I ended up preserving slide boundaries as the smallest retrieval unit rather than splitting by token count — losing some density but dramatically improving citation precision.

Deciding when to invoke vision was a cost/quality tradeoff. Sending every page to a vision model would be expensive and slow. I use a text-density heuristic: pages below a threshold get a Gemini vision pass; pages with dense prose stay text-only. This keeps ingestion costs bounded while still capturing the diagrams that actually matter.

Hybrid retrieval tuning. BM25 alone over-privileged rare-but-irrelevant technical tokens; vector search alone lost precision on exact terminology. RRF was the right merger — parameter-free and robust — but I still needed the cross-encoder to fix the top-3, which was where the LLM was most sensitive to ranking noise.

Preventing hallucinated citations. Even with retrieval-grounded prompts, LLMs will occasionally invent slide numbers. I added a post-generation validator that checks every citation in the answer against the retrieved chunks; anything that doesn't match gets stripped before the answer reaches the user.

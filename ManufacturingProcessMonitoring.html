Manufacturing Process Monitoring — Computer Vision for OEE

YOLOv12 PaddleOCR Jetson Orin Nano MQTT OEE Model Benchmarking

Problem Statement

Overall Equipment Effectiveness is the number a factory floor runs on, and computing it honestly needs something most shops do not have: a continuous, trustworthy record of what every machine was doing, minute by minute. Was it cutting? Idle and waiting for an operator? Mid tool-change? Being cleaned down between jobs?

The usual answers are all unsatisfying. Operators fill in logs by hand, which is retrospective and optimistic. Machine controllers can report state directly, but only if they are new enough and networked — and on a real floor, a fleet is a mix of vintages, vendors and controllers, many of which expose nothing at all. Replacing those machines to gain visibility is not an option.

Approach

At Conduit I built the monitoring system around what every machine does expose, regardless of age: its appearance and its screen. A camera watches the machine, a vision model classifies what state it is in, and a second model reads the text on the HMI panel. Nothing is retrofitted into the controller and nothing depends on the vendor opening up an API.

State classification runs on YOLOv12, chosen after benchmarking alternatives across the full set of machine states. The distinction that matters is not running versus stopped — it is telling apart the several different reasons a machine is not cutting, because they land in different places in an OEE calculation. A tool change is planned; an idle machine waiting on an operator is not. Getting those boundaries right is what moved monitoring accuracy up by 45 percent over the previous approach.

The States the Model Separates

  • Cutting — the productive state everything else is measured against.
  • Idle — powered and available, but nothing is happening.
  • Tool change — planned downtime, and it should not be counted like an unplanned stop.
  • Loading parts — setup at the start of a job.
  • Removing parts — teardown at the end of one.
  • Bed cleaning — chip and swarf clearing between jobs.

Separating loading, removing and cleaning matters because together they are where a surprising amount of the day goes. Rolled up as a single "stopped" bucket they look like one loss; separated, they are three different problems with three different fixes.

Reading the HMI Panel

The HMI carries information that is invisible from the outside: the active program, alarm codes, cycle counts. I fine-tuned PaddleOCR for those screens specifically, because general-purpose OCR does poorly on HMI typography, fixed-width status fields, glare and low-contrast industrial displays. With the panel read as structured text, a stoppage can be attributed rather than merely counted. OEEutilization metrics

Inference runs on a Jetson Orin Nano at the machines rather than on a central server. That keeps video local — only small state messages leave the device — and it means the line keeps being measured even when the network does not cooperate. Those messages are published over MQTT, which the plant already speaks, so utilization data lands in existing dashboards instead of a parallel system nobody checks.

The default layout is a camera per machine, which gives the cleanest view of the work area and the panel. Where machines sit close together, a single camera can cover several at once and the frame is split per machine — fewer devices to mount and maintain, at the cost of angle and resolution on each.

What the System Delivers

  1. 86.7% OEE, measured rather than estimated. Utilization comes from what the cameras actually observed, not from hand-written logs reconstructed at the end of a shift.
  2. 45% better monitoring accuracy. The gain came from benchmarking state models and picking the one that held up on the harder, in-between conditions.
  3. Downtime with a reason attached. Six states plus OCR on the panel turn a flat stop into an attributable event.
  4. Works on machines that expose nothing. Because the system observes from the outside, the same deployment covers old and new equipment alike.
  5. Real-time and local. Edge inference on the Jetson with MQTT ingestion keeps latency low, bandwidth small, and video inside the plant.

SKU-Agnostic Inventory Monitoring — Segment, Embed, Match

SAM DINO Embeddings Cosine Similarity Blob Filtering Zero-Training Onboarding Video Pipeline

Problem Statement

A detector trained to recognise products is trained to recognise those products. The moment the catalogue changes — a new line, a new supplier, a seasonal item, a repackaged box — the model is behind, and catching up means collecting images, labelling them, retraining and redeploying. Inventory does not wait for that cycle, and a system that needs it is permanently out of date.

At Aethrellegence I built the opposite property into the system: adding a SKU is an act of registration, not an act of training. Nothing in the vision pipeline is ever trained on the products it will be asked about.

Approach

The way out is to split the problem in two. Finding a product in a frame is a generic visual task, shared across every SKU. Deciding which product it is, is a lookup. Once the problem is cut that way, both halves can be solved with foundation models that were never shown the catalogue: SAM proposes the regions, DINO describes them, and cosine similarity does the identifying.

That is what makes the system SKU-agnostic in a strict sense. There are no product classes anywhere in the model stack. The catalogue lives as a set of reference embeddings — data the system reads at match time, not weights baked in at training time — so it can be extended or pruned while the pipeline keeps running untouched.

🎯 Design goal: onboarding a new SKU is registering its embeddings — no dataset, no labelling, no retraining, no redeploy.

Stage one — SAM, then a size filter. SAM segments anything, which is exactly the property that makes it usable here and exactly what makes its raw output noisy: shelf edges, reflections, hands, floor texture and shadow all come back as masks alongside the products. The size filter is what turns that into a candidate list. Blobs outside the plausible area range for an item are dropped before anything expensive happens, so the identification stage only ever sees credible regions.

Stage two — DINO embeddings and cosine matching. Each surviving blob is embedded with DINO and compared against the onboarded reference embeddings. DINO is a good fit because its self-supervised features separate objects by appearance without any notion of a label, which is what lets an unseen product be described meaningfully the first time it is registered.

Matching accepts a SKU at a cosine similarity of roughly 0.93 and above. That is a deliberately strict bar: packaging across a product family often differs by little more than a flavour band or a colour, and a loose threshold turns those into confident mislabels. Anything below the bar is reported as unidentified rather than forced into the nearest class, which keeps an unregistered item visible as a gap to fill instead of silently inflating another count.

What the System Delivers

  1. New SKUs without retraining. Onboarding stores reference embeddings. The models, thresholds and deployment stay exactly as they were.
  2. No product classes in the model stack. SAM and DINO are both used off the shelf, so the catalogue is configuration rather than a training artefact.
  3. Cheap rejection before expensive inference. Size filtering discards implausible SAM masks before embedding, keeping the matching stage focused.
  4. Honest unknowns. A blob below 0.93 similarity stays unidentified, instead of being absorbed into the closest SKU.
